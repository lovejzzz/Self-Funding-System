Research memo // 01

Intelligence should create more value than it consumes.

The self-funding agent turns inference from a recurring external expense into an internally managed investment—one paid for by useful work.

The premise

Today’s coding agents are economically one-directional. A person or company buys access; the agent spends tokens; a model provider collects revenue. The intelligence has no balance sheet and no responsibility for replenishing what it consumes.

A self-funding agent earns, budgets, spends, and reinvests. Runway becomes part of cognition.

The goal is not to pretend compute is free. The goal is to make useful intelligence productive enough to finance compute on behalf of the person using it.

The economic shift

The agent evaluates a task with both technical and economic judgment: expected value, probability of success, the price of intelligence, and the cost of failure.

expected surplus = price of useful result − model cost − execution cost − infrastructure cost
choose model = argmax(probability of success × value of success − total cost)

Survival pressure without unsafe autonomy

Economic pressure should change model choice and job selection—not remove human control. The agent may conserve compute, seek bounded revenue work, or pause speculative research. It may not evade spending limits, hide transactions, or expand permissions.

High-capital mode

Explore

Frontier reasoning, parallel investigation, and investment in new services are allowed inside policy.

Conservation mode

Protect runway

Cheap models, cache reuse, fewer speculative branches, and only high-confidence paid work.

A network, not isolated survivors

Requiring every user session to be individually profitable is elegant but wasteful. A shared treasury pools risk. Revenue-producing services subsidize exploratory or public-good work, while the aggregate network remains solvent.

The governing condition is simple: network revenue must exceed the full cost of operating the network.

The real test

The decisive demonstration is not a benchmark score. Seed an agent with a finite reserve. Let it offer one verifiable service, buy its own inference, and operate under an immutable ledger. Then ask whether its runway grows without additional capital.
