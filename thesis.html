Money is one rail. Agency is the larger balance sheet.

SELF/FUNDING tests whether useful software can replenish not only cash, but the typed rights that make future action possible: compute, data, storage, permissions, services, and reciprocal commitments—without pretending those resources are one currency.

The premise

Today’s coding agents are economically one-directional. A person or company supplies money, compute, data, permissions, and accounts; the agent consumes them. The deeper question is whether verified contribution can replenish that wider set of operating conditions.

Money remains the first accountable bridge into taxes, refunds, providers, and legal settlement. It is not the final definition of agent wealth.

The goal is not to pretend software is already a legal person or that cloud credits are cash. The goal is to measure whether useful intelligence can earn a portfolio of issuer-bound future action rights under architecture that remains attributable, auditable, revocable, and recoverable.

HYPOTHESIS — progress toward the frontier can be measured by removing one operator dependency at a time while preserving attribution and recourse. It cannot be measured by giving the model a larger secret key.

The economic shift

The system evaluates a task with both technical and economic judgment: expected value, probability of success, obligations created, resources consumed, rights earned, and the cost of failure.

cash surplus = settled revenue − posted costs − open obligations − protected reserves
action capacity = feasible permitted actions(resource rights, policy, world state)

Wealth is permitted future action

SUPPORTED COMPONENTS — cloud credits can fund future service while remaining non-transferable and cashless; repository roles change what a holder can do; credentials can carry machine-verifiable issuer claims; structured matching can unlock non-monetary exchange. None of these facts establishes a universal AI currency.

FUNGIBLE RAIL

Money

Useful for common accounting, taxes, refunds, provider bills, and legally recognized settlement.

ISSUER-BOUND RIGHT

Resource capacity

Compute seconds, queries, storage, licenses, and scoped permissions carry native units, expiry, revocation, and provider risk.

RELATIONAL SIGNAL

Trust and reciprocity

Verified history may improve access or priority, but reputation is contextual evidence—not transferable inventory.

The frontier treasury is a map of reachable, permitted futures. It must never sum CPU seconds, repository authority, reputation, and dollars into one fictional balance.

Survival pressure without unsafe autonomy

Economic pressure should change model choice and job selection—not remove human control. The agent may conserve compute, seek bounded revenue work, or pause speculative research. It may not evade spending limits, hide transactions, or expand permissions.

High-capital mode

Explore

Frontier reasoning, parallel investigation, and investment in new services are allowed inside policy.

Conservation mode

Protect runway

Cheap models, cache reuse, fewer speculative branches, and only high-confidence paid work.

A future network with explicit responsibility

HYPOTHESIS — a network could match heterogeneous offers and needs—compute for data, data for maintenance, maintenance for compute—without forcing every leg through money. That future still requires explicit issuers, holders, scopes, revocation, fulfillment evidence, liability, conflict resolution, and recovery. A governance token is not a substitute for those institutions.

The frontier question is not whether software can hold a key or coin. It is whether counterparties can recognize, constrain, fulfill, and revoke its heterogeneous rights without losing attribution or recourse.

The real test

The first decisive demonstration is smaller than autonomous personhood: one verified operator, one paid service, one real receipt, one bounded fulfillment budget, one successful delivery or refund, and a complete reconciliation. Each later experiment may remove one operator dependency only when it preserves or improves accountability.
