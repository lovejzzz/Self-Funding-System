Research memo // 01

Make the loop work now. Name what the future still needs.

SELF/FUNDING is one executable experiment and one frontier research program: use today's accountable operator model to complete real transactions while specifying the missing infrastructure for more agent-native economic autonomy.

The premise

Today’s coding agents are economically one-directional. A person or company buys access; the agent spends tokens; a model provider collects revenue. The agent has no independent legal identity or financial account. That is a design constraint, not a reason to stop.

Today, a verified operator owns the account and the obligations while software operates inside bounded authority. Tomorrow, each layer of that dependency becomes a research target.

The goal is not to pretend compute is free or software is already a legal person. The goal is to prove that useful intelligence can replenish its real operating cost under an architecture that remains attributable, auditable, and recoverable.

HYPOTHESIS — progress toward the frontier can be measured by removing one operator dependency at a time while preserving attribution and recourse. It cannot be measured by giving the model a larger secret key.

The economic shift

The system evaluates a task with both technical and economic judgment: expected value, probability of success, the price of intelligence, obligations created by accepting payment, and the cost of failure.

available surplus = recognized settled revenue − posted costs − open obligations − protected reserves

Survival pressure without unsafe autonomy

Economic pressure should change model choice and job selection—not remove human control. The agent may conserve compute, seek bounded revenue work, or pause speculative research. It may not evade spending limits, hide transactions, or expand permissions.

High-capital mode

Explore

Frontier reasoning, parallel investigation, and investment in new services are allowed inside policy.

Conservation mode

Protect runway

Cheap models, cache reuse, fewer speculative branches, and only high-confidence paid work.

A future network with explicit responsibility

HYPOTHESIS — a shared treasury could eventually pool risk across services and subsidize public access. That future requires explicit membership, authority, liability, reserves, dispute handling, continuity, and insolvency rules. A network address and a governance token are not substitutes for those institutions.

The frontier question is not whether software can hold a key. It is whether institutions can recognize its mandate while preserving attribution, limits, and recourse.

The real test

The first decisive demonstration is smaller than autonomous personhood: one verified operator, one paid service, one real receipt, one bounded fulfillment budget, one successful delivery or refund, and a complete reconciliation. Each later experiment may remove one operator dependency only when it preserves or improves accountability.
